## Executive Summary & The Architecture of the 0.778+ Breakthrough

In October 2026, models trained exclusively on the competition's supplied application table (without external joins, without prohibited Kaggle bureau tables, and without test label leakage) reached verified local out-of-fold AUCs of **0.7739-0.7741** and public leaderboard scores of **0.77746, 0.77865, 0.77884, and 0.7789**.

This forensic audit reveals why recent clean-room experiments hovered at ~0.7658 while the October heritage reached ~0.778+:
1. **The Decoded Product / Tariff Space ($Q_0$ and $F$):**
   The loan parameters AMT_ANNUITY, AMT_CREDIT, and AMT_GOODS_PRICE are not independent continuous variables; they are generated by a discrete bank pricing engine. On ~75% of cash loans, a monthly service fee $F \approx 129.50$ is levied. The decoded interest rate parameter is:
   $$Q_0 = \frac{\text{AMT\_ANNUITY} - 129.50}{\text{AMT\_CREDIT}}$$
   Rounding $Q_0$ and raw $Q = \text{AMT\_ANNUITY}/\text{AMT\_CREDIT}$ to 3, 4, and 5 decimals isolates 1,047 discrete loan products.
2. **Leakage-Safe Fold-Local Target Encodings with Bayesian Smoothing:**
   Standard tree splits cannot partition continuous floating-point numbers into 1,047 narrow pricing intervals without severe fragmentation. The October pipeline applied fold-local Bayesian target encodings:
   $$\text{TE}(x) = \frac{\sum y_i + M_0 \cdot \bar{y}}{N_x + M_0}, \quad M_0 \in \{30, 100\}$$
   computed strictly inside training folds and mapped onto validation and test folds.
3. **Randomized Split Thresholds (`extra_trees=True`) in Gradient Boosting:**
   Discrete product encodings are high-cardinality and prone to overfitting standard greedy tree splitters. By enabling `extra_trees=True` in LightGBM, split thresholds are selected uniformly at random across candidate thresholds, providing massive structural regularization (+16.9 bps OOF gain across all 5 folds confirmed on the live dataset).
4. **16 Learned Expectation (Residual) Features via `HistGradientBoostingRegressor`:**
   Rather than manual heuristic arithmetic, 16 continuous attributes (including external scores, credit amounts, and employment length) were predicted conditional on all other features. The expectation residuals $RR_k = X_k - \hat{X}_k$ expose individual-level deviations from demographic and portfolio peer groups.
5. **Multi-View Stacking ($E_1$) Across Domain Partitions:**
   Instead of forcing a single tree model to balance external bureau risk against pricing structure and personal demographics, three separate `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` models were fit on disjoint feature views:
   - **View 1 (`EXT`):** External bureau scores and non-linear interactions.
   - **View 2 (`LOAN`):** Loan terms, tariffs, product keys, and affordability ratios.
   - **View 3 (`PERSON`):** Age, employment, education, housing, and family stability.
   The resulting out-of-fold logit predictions and cross-view discrepancies (e.g. $\text{VIEW\_EXT} - \text{VIEW\_LOAN}$) served as meta-features for downstream gradient boosters.
6. **Cross-Architecture Blending & Greedy Rank Hill-Climbing:**
   Five diverse LightGBM models (`qf_shallow`, `qf_reg`, `v1_allfam_oh`, `v2_allfam_oh`, `v2_mono`) combined with CatBoost and HistGradientBoosting regressors, ensembled via out-of-fold Nelder-Mead and nested greedy hill-climbing (`climb`).

### 2.3 Greedy Hill-Climbing Ensembling (`climb`, Cell 7)
Rather than simple arithmetic averaging, weights are optimized using a coordinate-wise greedy hill-climb over rank-transformed predictions:
```python
def climb(rows, iters=40):
    cnt = {c: 0 for c in COMPS}; cur = np.zeros(n)
    for _ in range(iters):
        best = max(COMPS, key=lambda c: fast_auc(cur + O[c], rows))
        cnt[best] += 1
        cur = cur + O[best]
    return {c: v / iters for c, v in cnt.items() if v}
```
Evaluating weights inside a nested 5-fold CV (`StratifiedKFold(5, shuffle=True, random_state=3)`) strictly prevents ensembling leakage.
