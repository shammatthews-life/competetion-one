## 2. Structural Solvency & Distance-to-Default (Merton, 1974)

Credit bureau ratings alone fail to capture cash-flow insolvency when applicants have high external scores but excessive contractual debt burdens. We model structural solvency via Robert C. Merton's structural default theory adapted for retail consumer debt.

### 2.1 Disposable Cash Flow & Structural Horizon
Let:
- $C_i = \text{AMT\_CREDIT}_i$ (Principal loan amount)
- $A_i = \text{AMT\_ANNUITY}_i$ (Monthly loan payment obligation)
- $Y_i = \text{AMT\_INCOME\_TOTAL}_i$ (Reported annual applicant income)

The **monthly disposable cash flow coverage ratio** $D_i$ is defined as:
$$D_i = \max\left(0.01, \frac{Y_i / 12.0}{A_i + 1.0}\right)$$

The **effective contractual maturity** $T_i$ (in annual units) is given by:
$$T_i = \text{clip}\left(\frac{C_i / (A_i + 10^{-5})}{12.0}, 0.1, 10.0\right)$$

### 2.2 Distance-to-Default ($DD$) and Default Probability ($PD$)
Assuming the applicant's disposable cash flow follows a geometric Brownian motion with drift $\mu$ and volatility $\sigma$:
$$d \ln D_t = \left(\mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)dt + \sigma dW_t$$

The normalized **Distance-to-Default** $\text{DD}_i$ is:
$$\text{DD}_i(\mu, \sigma) = \frac{\ln(D_i) + \left(\mu - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right) T_i}{\sigma \sqrt{T_i}}$$

The **Merton Structural Probability of Default** $\text{PD}_i$ is the cumulative standard normal distribution evaluated at $-\text{DD}_i$:
$$\text{PD}_i(\mu, \sigma) = \Phi(-\text{DD}_i) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-\infty}^{-\text{DD}_i} e^{-\frac{u^2}{2}} du$$

We evaluate two distinct macroeconomic drift/volatility parameterizations:
1. **Conservative Regime:** $\mu_1 = 0.02$, $\sigma_1 = 0.20 \implies \text{MERTON\_DD\_20}, \text{MERTON\_PD\_20}$
2. **Stress Regime:** $\mu_2 = 0.03$, $\sigma_2 = 0.25 \implies \text{MERTON\_DD\_25}, \text{MERTON\_PD\_25}$

## 3. Clayton Archimedean Copulas for Lower-Tail Multi-Agency Collapse

External credit bureaus ($\text{EXT}_1, \text{EXT}_2, \text{EXT}_3$) exhibit asymmetric lower-tail dependency: when an applicant experiences severe liquidity distress, all three bureau scores tend to collapse simultaneously, whereas high bureau scores exhibit much lower cross-agency correlation.

### 3.1 Empirical Margin Transformation
To avoid distributional bias, continuous marginals are transformed to uniform variables $u \in [0, 1]$ using the empirical cumulative distribution function fitted strictly on the training partition $\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}$:
$$u_{j, i} = \frac{\sum_{k \in \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}} \mathbf{1}_{\{\text{EXT}_{j, k} \le \text{EXT}_{j, i}\}} + 0.5}{|\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}| + 1.0}, \quad \text{for } j \in \{1, 2, 3\}$$

Missing scores are imputed to the neutral median percentile $u_{j, i} = 0.5$.

### 3.2 Clayton Trivariate Archimedean Copula
The Clayton Archimedean generator is:
$$\psi(t) = \frac{1}{\theta} (t^{-\theta} - 1), \quad \psi^{-1}(s) = (1 + \theta s)^{-1/\theta}$$

The 3-variate lower-tail copula function is:
$$C_\theta(u_1, u_2, u_3) = \max\left(u_1^{-\theta} + u_2^{-\theta} + u_3^{-\theta} - 2.0, 0\right)^{-1/\theta}$$

The lower-tail dependence coefficient is given by:
$$\lambda_L = 2^{-1/\theta}$$

We extract joint collapse indices across two distinct tail-coupling intensities:
- $\theta = 1.5 \implies \lambda_L = 2^{-0.667} \approx 0.630$ (`CLAYTON_COPULA_1_5`)
- $\theta = 2.5 \implies \lambda_L = 2^{-0.400} \approx 0.758$ (`CLAYTON_COPULA_2_5`)

## 4. Contractual Implicit Interest Rate Decoder (Bisection APR Solver)

The dataset omits the contractual interest rate. However, using the financial annuity equation for a fixed-rate installment loan, the true implicit annual percentage rate (APR) is encoded in the non-linear relationship between Credit ($C$), Annuity ($A$), and Term ($n$).

### 4.1 Fixed Installment Annuity Equation
For principal $C$, monthly installment $A$, term $n$ months, and monthly interest rate $r$:
$$A = C \cdot \frac{r}{1 - (1 + r)^{-n}}$$

Rearranging gives the non-linear root-finding problem $f(r) = 0$:
$$f(r) = C \cdot r - A \left(1 - (1 + r)^{-n}\right) = 0$$

### 4.2 Numerical Bisection Solver
For each applicant $i$ and potential term $n \in \{6, 12, 18, 24, 30, 36, 48, 60\}$:
$$\text{Bracket: } r_{\text{lo}} = 10^{-7}, \quad r_{\text{hi}} = 0.50$$
Iterating $k = 1, \dots, 30$:
$$r_{\text{mid}} = \frac{r_{\text{lo}} + r_{\text{hi}}}{2.0}$$
$$\text{If } f(r_{\text{mid}}) > 0 \implies r_{\text{hi}} = r_{\text{mid}}, \quad \text{else } r_{\text{lo}} = r_{\text{mid}}$$

The annual rate is:
$$\text{APR}_n = 12.0 \times r_{\text{mid}}$$

### 4.3 Optimal Term & Rate Selection
The optimal contractual term $n^*$ is selected by minimizing distance to standard consumer bank APR (25% APR prior):
$$n^* = \arg\min_{n \in \{6..60\}, \text{APR}_n \in [0.02, 0.90]} |\text{APR}_n - 0.25|$$
$$\text{RATE\_BEST}_i = \text{APR}_{n^*}$$

### 4.4 Contract Pricing Expectation Residual
A ridge regressor estimates the expected rate given credit amount and bureau mean:
$$\widehat{r}_i = \mathbf{w}^T \left[\overline{\text{EXT}}_i, \ln(C_i)\right] + b$$
$$\text{RATE\_RESID}_i = \text{RATE\_BEST}_i - \widehat{r}_i$$

$$\text{Pay} = \frac{A}{C + 10^{-5}}, \quad \text{CTI} = \frac{C}{Y + 10^{-5}}, \quad \text{ATI} = \frac{A}{Y + 10^{-5}}$$
$$\overline{\text{EXT}} = \frac{1}{3}\sum_{j=1}^3 \text{EXT}_j, \quad \text{EXT}_{\min} = \min(\text{EXT}_j), \quad \text{EXT}_{\max} = \max(\text{EXT}_j)$$
$$\text{Age} = -\frac{\text{DAYS\_BIRTH}}{365.25}, \quad \text{EmpYears} = \max\left(0, -\frac{\text{DAYS\_EMPLOYED}}{365.25}\right)$$

## 7. Decoded Product Key Manifold ($K_{\text{dec}}$ & 17 Product Keys)

Underwriters utilize discrete interest schedules. These are recovered via financial reverse-engineering:

### 7.1 Installment Surcharge Counter $K_{\text{dec}}$
Let $G_i = \text{AMT\_GOODS\_PRICE}_i$. The credit markup ratio is:
$$R_{\text{CG}} = \frac{C_i}{G_i + 10^{-5}}$$

Empirical underwriting quantization discovers a 1.32% step increment:
$$k_{\text{float}} = \frac{R_{\text{CG}} - 1.0}{0.0132}$$
$$K_{\text{dec}} = \begin{cases} \text{round}(k_{\text{float}}) & \text{if } |k_{\text{float}} - \text{round}(k_{\text{float}})| < 0.10 \\ 0.0 & \text{if Cash loan and } C_i = G_i \\ \text{NaN} & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

### 7.2 Base Ratio & Surcharge Fee Indicator
$$q = \frac{A}{C}, \quad q_b = \frac{A - 129.5}{C}$$
$$\text{FEE} = \mathbf{1}_{\{\text{freq}(\text{round}(q_b, 5)) > \text{freq}(\text{round}(q, 5)) \text{ and Cash loan}\}}$$
$$Q_0 = \begin{cases} q_b & \text{if } \text{FEE} = 1 \\ q & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

The 17 product keys ($Q_3, Q_4, Q_5, P_3, P_4, P_5, PF_4, CT_1, CT_2, CT_3$, etc.) are constructed by string discretization.

## 8. In-Fold Bayesian Target Encoding with Exact Smoothing

To prevent target leakage while extracting categorical default density:

### 8.1 Empirical Bayes Posterior Formulation
For a training partition $\mathcal{F}_{\text{fit}} \subset \mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}$ with global default prior $p_0 = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{F}_{\text{fit}}|} \sum_{i \in \mathcal{F}_{\text{fit}}} y_i$:
For category level $c$ of key $K$, with observed count $N_c = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{F}_{\text{fit}}} \mathbf{1}_{\{K_i = c\}}$ and defaults $S_c = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{F}_{\text{fit}}} y_i \mathbf{1}_{\{K_i = c\}}$:

The Bayesian posterior expected target value under a Beta prior with prior weight $M_K$ is:
$$\text{TE}_K(c) = \frac{S_c + M_K \cdot p_0}{N_c + M_K}$$

### 8.2 Smoothing Constants ($M_K$)
- High-cardinality keys ($Q_3, P_3$): $M_K = 100.0$
- Medium keys ($Q_4, Q_5, P_4, P_5, PF_4$): $M_K = 30.0$
- Ratio keys ($CT_1, CT_2, CT_3$, amounts): $M_K = 50.0$
- Fine smoothing: $M_K = 10.0$; Broad smoothing: $M_K = 200.0$

For training instances, $\text{TE}$ values are computed via an internal 5-fold cross-validation split of $\mathcal{F}_{\text{fit}}$, guaranteeing that row $i$'s label is never used in computing $\text{TE}(K_i)$.

## 9. Complete Arithmetic Leave-One-Out (LOO) Self-Exclusion

For any cohort grouping key $G$ (e.g. `ORGANIZATION_TYPE`, `OCCUPATION_TYPE`) and continuous metric $V$ (e.g. `AMT_INCOME_TOTAL`, `EXT_SOURCE_2`):

### 9.1 Training Partition Self-Exclusion
Let $S_G = \sum_{j \in G} V_j$ and $N_G = \sum_{j \in G} \mathbf{1}_{\{V_j \text{ is not NaN}\}}$.
For training applicant $i \in G$:
$$N_{G, -i} = \max(N_G - \mathbf{1}_{\{V_i \text{ is not NaN}\}}, 1.0)$$
$$S_{G, -i} = S_G - V_i \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\{V_i \text{ is not NaN}\}}$$
$$\mu_{G, -i} = \frac{S_{G, -i}}{N_{G, -i}}$$
$$\Delta V_i = V_i - \mu_{G, -i}$$

### 9.2 Test Partition Reference Mapping
For test applicant $k \in G$:
$$\mu_G = \frac{S_G}{N_G}, \quad \Delta V_k = V_k - \mu_G$$

Zero test statistics leak into training distributions.

## 11. Multi-View Meta-Learner Manifold

Features are partitioned into orthogonal information views:
1. $\mathcal{V}_{\text{EXT}}$: External agency triad, copulas, and polynomial EXT interactions.
2. $\mathcal{V}_{\text{LOAN}}$: Credit, annuity, goods, terms, implicit APR, and solvency ratios.
3. $\mathcal{V}_{\text{PERSON}}$: Identity invariants, demographic age, and registration variables.
4. $\mathcal{V}_{\text{EXTAGE}}$: Joint age-credit bureau interaction manifold.
5. $\mathcal{V}_{\text{NOEXT}}$: Completely bureau-independent underwriting manifold.
6. $\mathcal{V}_{\text{GROUPS}}$: Cohort demographic differential manifold.

### 11.1 Logit Meta-Predictions
For each view $v \in \mathcal{V}$, a regularized classifier outputs probability $p_v$:
$$\text{VIEW}_v = \ln\left(\frac{p_v}{1.0 - p_v}\right)$$

### 11.2 Pairwise Logit Differential Space
$$\text{VD}_{\text{EL}} = \text{VIEW}_{\text{EXT}} - \text{VIEW}_{\text{LOAN}}$$
$$\text{VD}_{\text{EP}} = \text{VIEW}_{\text{EXT}} - \text{VIEW}_{\text{PERSON}}$$
$$\text{VD}_{\text{LP}} = \text{VIEW}_{\text{LOAN}} - \text{VIEW}_{\text{PERSON}}$$

- **Bagging Seeds:** $\{42, 7\}$ on full dataset for maximum variance reduction.
- **Specification Weights:**
  $$W = \{0.24, 0.12, 0.32, 0.16, 0.16\}$$
- **Dual-Stream Assembly:**
  $$B_{17} = \frac{1}{3} \left[\text{blend}(\text{old}) + \text{blend}(\text{e1}) + \text{blend}(\text{e1et})\right]$$
  $$v_{17} = 0.75 \cdot \text{rk}(B_{17}) + 0.25 \cdot \text{blend}(\text{e1Aet})$$
  $$B_{18} = \frac{1}{3} \left[\text{blend}(\text{old}) + \text{blend}(\text{e1}) + \text{blend}(\text{e1et\_v18})\right]$$

## 13. Convex Rank Fusion & Non-Negative Stacking

Let $\text{rk}(s)$ be the normalized uniform rank function:
$$\text{rk}(s) = \frac{\text{rank}(s) - 1.0}{|s| - 1.0} \in [0.0, 1.0]$$

### 13.1 Tri-Engine Master Fusion (V16)
$$P_{\text{V16}} = \text{rk}\left(0.85 \cdot \text{rk}(P_{\text{LGB}}) + 0.08 \cdot \text{rk}(P_{\text{CB}}) + 0.07 \cdot \text{rk}(P_{\text{XGB}})\right)$$
*Result:* Achieved all-time high pooled OOF AUC of **`0.772969`** (XGBoost GPU Fold 3 reached **`0.77714`**).

### 13.2 Grand Master Convex Blend (V17)
$$P_{\text{V17}} = \text{rk}\left(0.70 \cdot \text{rk}(P_{\text{V11}}) + 0.30 \cdot \text{rk}(P_{\text{V16}})\right)$$
*Result:* Achieved record diagnostic rank correlation of **`99.0361%`** vs the 0.78200 benchmark and **`98.8811%`** vs the 0.78239 benchmark.

### 13.3 Non-Negative Probit Stacker Formulation
For meta-learning across ensemble streams, probit-transformed rank vectors are stacked:
$$z_{m, i} = \Phi^{-1}\left(\text{clip}\left(\text{rk}(P_{m, i}), 10^{-6}, 1 - 10^{-6}\right)\right)$$

The optimal non-negative stacking weights $\mathbf{w}^* \in \mathbb{R}_+^M$ and intercept $b$ minimize regularized binary cross-entropy:
$$\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{w}, b) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \left[\ln\left(1 + e^{\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{z}_i + b}\right) - y_i \left(\mathbf{w}^T \mathbf{z}_i + b\right)\right] + \lambda \|\mathbf{w}\|_2^2$$
$$\text{subject to } w_m \ge 0 \quad \forall m \in \{1, \dots, M\}$$

Solved via bounded L-BFGS-B optimization.
